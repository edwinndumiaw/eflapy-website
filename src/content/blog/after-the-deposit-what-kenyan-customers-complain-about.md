When a company in Kenya gets a bad Google review, the first instinct is to blame the product: the house was late, the claim was rejected, the hotel was not what the client expected. We wanted to know whether that is really what customers complain about. So we read what they wrote.

## What we did

We looked at the public Google reviews of 16 Nairobi companies across three sectors where clients pay large amounts before they receive everything they paid for: six property developers, five insurers and five tour operators. We read every review shown for each company and sorted what customers praised and what they complained about.

This is a first look, not a census. But the pattern was consistent enough across all three sectors that it is worth sharing now. We are not naming any company. The point is not who got it wrong, but what keeps going wrong.

## The finding: complaints start when communication stops

Across all three sectors, the angriest reviews were rarely about the product alone. They were about silence.

**Property developers.** The most detailed complaint came from a diaspora investor. Before paying, they received regular updates and quick answers. After a deposit of more than KES 2.5 million and a signed sale agreement, the updates stopped, and repeated questions about the project went unanswered for months. Another review described buyers still waiting for title deeds years after handover, with nobody giving them a straight answer.

**Insurers.** Most bad reviews were about what happens after a claim is made. Customers wrote that the people responsible never pick up their phones, that emails take far too long to get a reply, and that promised callbacks never come. One customer had an approved claim and still could not get the refund or a clear update.

**Tour operators.** This sector told the same story from the other side. The highly rated operators were praised again and again for one thing: they replied quickly and kept the client informed before and during the trip. One traveller wrote that they compared three quotes and chose the company only because its consultant was the fastest and most helpful to respond.

Put together, the lesson is simple. A customer will forgive a delay they were told about. They will not forgive being ignored after they have paid.

## Why it happens inside the company

From the outside, silence looks like a company that does not care. From the inside, it usually looks like this:

- Client messages arrive on WhatsApp, email, phone calls, Instagram and the website, and land in different people's phones and inboxes.
- Nobody clearly owns each client after the sale is closed. Sales has moved on to the next deal, and operations does not know what was promised.
- Updates depend on someone remembering to send them.
- Managers find out a client is unhappy when the review is already public.

None of this is about bad staff. It is about a business that has grown beyond what phones, personal inboxes and memory can hold.

## What the best rated companies do differently

The companies with strong ratings were not perfect. Some had delays and problems too. What set them apart was that the client always knew what was happening.

- **They reply fast, on any channel.** The first reply comes in minutes, not days.
- **One named person owns the client.** Reviews mention the consultant or coordinator by name.
- **Updates are planned, not remembered.** The client hears from the company at every stage, especially after paying.
- **Problems are explained before the client has to ask.**

## Five questions for decision makers

Ask your team these, or check for yourself:

1. If a client sends a WhatsApp message at 7pm on a Saturday, when do they get a reply, and from whom?
2. After a client pays a deposit or buys a policy, what updates do they receive, and when?
3. Can you see every conversation your team has had with one client in one place?
4. When a staff member leaves, does the client history stay with the company?
5. Do you find out a client is unhappy before or after they post a review?

If several answers are "it depends who handles it", the problem is not your people. It is your system.

## Where a CRM changes this

A modern CRM is not a contact list. Set up properly, it is the system your whole client relationship runs on, and it fixes every cause of silence described above.

**Every channel in one inbox.** WhatsApp, SMS, email, phone calls, Instagram, Facebook Messenger, Google Business chat and your website chat all arrive in one place. Your team replies from there, and managers can see every conversation and how long each client waited.

**AI that answers instantly, under your control.** Conversational AI can reply to a new enquiry within seconds, at any hour, using your own information: prices, project details, policy terms, itineraries. You choose how much it does on its own. It can reply directly, or it can draft each answer for a team member to approve with one tap. Either way, no client waits until Monday.

**Nurturing, not just follow up.** A good system does more than remind someone to call back. It runs planned journeys that match how your clients buy. A property buyer who enquired but did not book a site visit gets project updates, a payment plan explanation and an invitation to the next open day, spread over the following weeks. A client who has paid a deposit gets a progress update at every construction stage, automatically. A traveller gets a checklist a week before the trip and a check in on the first evening. Each message goes at the right moment, in your voice.

**A full profile for every client.** Every call, message, payment, document and note sits on one record. Anyone who picks up the conversation knows what was promised.

**A pipeline that shows where every client stands.** From enquiry to deposit to handover or claim settlement, you can see who is waiting, for how long, and who is responsible.

**Reputation managed on purpose.** Review requests go out after a good experience. Every Google and Facebook review lands in one feed, with replies drafted for your team to approve. An unhappy client is flagged early, so the conversation happens privately before it happens in public.

**A dashboard for leadership.** Response times, open conversations, deals in progress and review ratings, live, without waiting for a weekly report.
